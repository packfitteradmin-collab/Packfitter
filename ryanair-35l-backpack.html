    Airline enforcement

    How Strict Is Ryanair With Carry-On Bags?

    Ryanair is one of the strictest airlines in the world for carry-on enforcement. Every passenger must place their bag in a physical sizer box at the gate — if it doesn't fit, you pay a fee and it goes in the hold. The free allowance is one small personal bag (40 × 30 × 20 cm) that fits under the seat. Anything larger — including most backpacks over 20L — requires Priority Boarding (a paid upgrade), which allows a 55 × 40 × 20 cm cabin bag in the overhead bin. The 20 cm (7.9 in) depth limit is the binding constraint. Most backpacks exceed 20 cm (7.9 in) when packed, even if height and width are within range. Ryanair charges a gate fee for non-compliant bags that is much higher than the Priority Boarding upgrade costs. Weight is enforced: 10 kg for cabin bags. Gate agents use handheld scales. There is no soft enforcement here. Ryanair staff are trained to catch oversized bags and the airline profits from gate fees. If you're flying Ryanair, buy Priority Boarding and measure your bag before you leave home.

    35L Backpack on Ryanair: What Actually Changes?

    A 35L backpack is the practical carry-on ceiling on Ryanair. It requires Priority Boarding and must fit the 55 × 40 × 20 cm sizer. At 35L, most travel packs exceed 20 cm (7.9 in) depth when fully packed. The bag must be underpacked or heavily compressed to clear. Ryanair's sizer box enforcement is not flexible — if the bag doesn't slide in smoothly, it's flagged. Travelers who make 35L work on Ryanair typically use compression cubes and leave 15–20% of the bag empty. Beyond 5 days, you'll need laundry access to stay within this constraint.

    How Accurate Is This?

    Ryanair's free cabin item is 40×30×20 cm (under-seat); paid priority boarding adds a 55×40×20 cm (21.7 × 15.7 × 7.9 in) carry-on to the overhead bin. The dimensions translate roughly to 16×12×8 inches (free) and 22×16×8 inches (paid). Enforcement is extremely strict—sizers are deployed at every gate, and oversized bags incur a gate fee on the spot that is much higher than pre-booking.

    A 30L or 40L backpack will not fit Ryanair's free allowance and requires purchase of priority boarding (a paid add-on, cheaper pre-booked than at the gate). Without priority, your larger bag must be checked, which carries additional fees. Ryanair's low fares come with tight packing expectations and strict dimensional compliance.

    Unlike US carriers, Ryanair does not negotiate bag size at the gate. If your bag sizes exceed the sizer slot, you pay the fee or gate-check immediately. Ryanair publishes a 10 kg cabin bag weight limit. Enforcement varies by airport and gate staff — dimensions are checked more consistently than weight, but don't assume weight checks will be skipped.

    This is a planning tool to estimate capacity; actual gate acceptance depends on strict dimensional compliance and priority boarding purchase. For larger backpacks, factor priority boarding cost into your Ryanair fare calculation.
