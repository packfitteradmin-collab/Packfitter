Packing Guide — Caribbean Cruise in December

What to Pack for a Caribbean Cruise in December

Last updated: August 2026

December is peak Caribbean cruise season — dry-season sunshine, daytime temperatures around 75–85°F, calmer seas, and hurricane season already behind you by mid-month. The packing itself stays light and warm-weather easy, but December adds a layer summer months don't: the holidays. Formal and festive nights are more common over the season, evenings on deck feel a touch cooler, and if your sailing crosses Christmas or New Year's you'll want dressier outfits — and maybe a few gifts — alongside the swimwear and sunscreen. This guide covers exactly what changes in December, what most people forget, and how to fit it all in a carry-on if you want to skip the luggage wait.

Quick Answer

What Should I Pack for a Caribbean Cruise in December?

Pack warm-weather basics plus two December-specific additions: a light layer for cooler evenings and a dressier outfit for holiday and formal nights. Days are sunny and 75–85°F; the dry season is beginning, so rain is less frequent than fall. Carry-on (28–40L) is very achievable — the top forgotten items are reef-safe sunscreen, water shoes, a light evening layer, and one festive or formal outfit for the holidays.

Quick Decision

Can You Do a December Caribbean Cruise with Just a Carry-On?

Yes — Caribbean cruises are the easiest type to do carry-on only, and December is no exception. Warm-weather clothing is light and compact, so a 30–40L bag handles a 7-day sailing for disciplined packers using compression cubes. The December wrinkle is the holidays: a formal or festive outfit, dressier shoes, and any wrapped-onboard gifts add volume that summer packers skip. If you're sailing over Christmas or New Year's and want multiple dressy looks, or you simply prefer not to ration outfits, plan for a checked bag — plenty of Caribbean cruisers do.

    December Caribbean Cruise Packing List

    Tops
    5–7 lightweight tops
    Tank tops, t-shirts, and linen or moisture-wicking shirts. Quick-dry fabrics beat cotton in Caribbean humidity, and most tops re-wear once after a few hours of air-drying.
    Bottoms
    2–3 shorts, skirts, or light pants
    Shorts handle most days. One pair of light pants or a maxi skirt covers cooler December evenings and excursions where longer coverage helps.
    Swimwear
    2 swimsuits + 1 cover-up
    Two suits let one dry while you wear the other. Water stays warm (around 80°F) in December, so pool and beach time is in full swing — pack one suit in your embarkation-day bag.
    Light Evening Layer
    1 cardigan, wrap, or light sweater
    December's biggest difference from summer: evenings feel cooler, especially on deck with the sea breeze, and ship air conditioning runs cold. A light layer you can stuff in a sling covers both.
    Holiday / Formal Wear
    1–2 dressy or festive outfits
    Most December sailings have 1–2 formal or elegant nights, plus holiday events — Christmas dinners, festive theme nights, and a New Year's Eve gala on late-December cruises. One versatile dressy outfit covers a standard formal night; add a second festive option if you're sailing over the holidays.
    Underwear & Socks
    5–7 underwear, 2–3 pairs socks
    Fewer socks than usual — you'll be in sandals or barefoot most of the trip. Socks mainly for walking shoes on excursion days and dressier evenings.
    Footwear
    Sandals (worn) + water shoes (packed)
    Sandals or flip-flops cover pool decks, the ship, and casual port walking. Water shoes or sport sandals with grip are essential for beach excursions — many Caribbean ports have coral or rocky entry points. Add dressier sandals or loafers if your line enforces a formal code.
    Sun Protection
    Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50+), sunglasses, hat
    December sun is still strong — the UV index runs high year-round in the Caribbean. Some destinations restrict sunscreens with reef-harming ingredients — Aruba restricts oxybenzone, and Bonaire restricts oxybenzone and octinoxate — so check your itinerary; a mineral sunscreen is a simple choice. Bring your own either way, as ship shops charge a steep markup. A wide-brim hat and polarized sunglasses round out sun defense.
    Rain Prep
    Compact packable rain jacket or poncho
    December is the start of the dry season, but brief tropical showers still pass through. A packable rain layer takes almost no space and saves a soggy walk back to the ship.
    Daypack / Excursion Gear
    Small sling or daypack (8–15L)
    Doubles as your embarkation-day bag and port kit. Big enough for sunscreen, water, a phone, a light layer, and a waterproof pouch; light enough to carry all day in the heat.

    Power & Tech
    Phone charger + power bank
    Cabin outlets are limited. A power bank keeps your phone alive on full-day port excursions. Cruise cabins prohibit surge-protected power strips — bring a non-surge outlet adapter if you need extra plugs.
    Documents & Meds
    Passport/ID, cruise documents, motion sickness meds, waterproof phone pouch
    Keep documents in your embarkation-day bag, never in checked luggage. Caribbean seas are generally calm in December, but small-boat excursions and tender ports can still trigger motion sickness — have relief accessible before you need it.

    Holiday Extras (optional)
    Festive outfit, small gifts, gift bags
    On Christmas and New Year's sailings, many travelers bring an ugly sweater for theme nights and small gifts for family aboard. Don't wrap gifts in advance — security may unwrap them; pack flat gift bags and wrap onboard.

    Common Questions

    December Caribbean Cruise Packing FAQ

    What should I pack for a Caribbean cruise in December?
    Warm-weather basics — 5–7 light tops, 2–3 shorts or skirts, 2 swimsuits, a cover-up, sandals, water shoes, and reef-safe sunscreen — plus two December additions: a light layer for cooler evenings and a dressier outfit for holiday and formal nights. Most December setups fit in 28–40L; holiday dresswear and gifts are what push some travelers to check a bag.
    How warm is the Caribbean in December?
    Days average about 75–85°F with strong sun, and water stays around 80°F. December begins the dry season — hurricane season officially ends November 30 — so rain is less frequent than fall. Evenings feel cooler than the daytime peak, especially on deck, which is why a light layer earns its place in December.
    Do December Caribbean cruises have formal or holiday nights?
    Yes. Alongside the usual 1–2 formal nights, December sailings add holiday events — Christmas dinners, festive theme nights, ugly-sweater parties, and a New Year's Eve gala on late-December cruises (a formal night on nearly every line). Pack at least one versatile dressy outfit, and a second festive option if you're sailing over the holidays.
    Is December a good time for a Caribbean cruise, and is it rainy?
    December is one of the most popular months: hurricane season is over, humidity drops, seas are calmer, and the dry season begins. The trade-off is crowds and price — holiday-week sailings are among the busiest and most expensive of the year. Rain is possible but usually brief; a packable rain jacket handles the occasional shower.
    Can I do a December Caribbean cruise with just a carry-on?
    Yes — Caribbean cruises are the easiest type to do carry-on only, because warm-weather clothing is light. A 30–40L bag works for a 7-day December sailing with compression cubes. The caveat is the holidays: a formal or festive outfit, dressier shoes, and wrapped-onboard gifts add volume. If you want multiple dressy looks over Christmas or New Year's, plan for a checked bag.

    Bottom Line

    • December is peak Caribbean season — warm (75–85°F), dry, and calmer, with hurricane season already over
    • The packing base stays light and warm-weather easy — this is one of the lowest-volume cruise types
    • Two December additions matter: a light layer for cooler evenings and a dressier outfit for holiday and formal nights
    • Late-December sailings mean a New Year's Eve gala and dressier expectations — pack a second festive or formal option
    • Reef-safe sunscreen, water shoes, and a packable rain jacket are the most-forgotten items
    • Carry-on only (30–40L) is very doable; holiday dresswear and gifts are what push some travelers to check
    • Don't wrap gifts in advance — pack flat gift bags and wrap onboard
