Packing Guide — Caribbean Cruise in February

What to Pack for a Caribbean Cruise in February

Last updated: August 2026

February is peak Caribbean cruise season — and for good reason. It's among the warmest, driest, and calmest months of the year, with low-to-mid 80s days, low humidity, and generally smooth seas. The trade-off is crowds and price: Presidents' Day week, Valentine's, and winter-weary travelers make February the busiest, priciest month to sail. Packing stays warm-weather easy, a touch lighter than January since the evenings are milder — but the ship's air conditioning still calls for one light layer. This guide covers what February changes, what most people forget, and how to fit it all in a carry-on.

Quick Answer

What Should I Pack for a Caribbean Cruise in February?

Pack warm-weather basics plus one light layer for the ship's air conditioning and the occasional cool evening — a wrap or light cardigan, not a real jacket. Days are warm and dry at low-to-mid 80s, and seas are calm. Standard sailings have one or two formal nights, with a Valentine's cruise leaning slightly dressier. The most forgotten items are a light layer, reef-safe sunscreen, and water shoes.

Quick Decision

Can You Do a February Caribbean Cruise with Just a Carry-On?

Yes — easily, for most packers. February is very light warm-weather packing: shorts, swimwear, and just one thin layer for the air conditioning, since evenings are milder than the deeper winter months. A 35–40L carry-on handles a 7-day sailing with room to spare for most travelers, with most setups packing into ~28–35L. The peak-season crowds don't change what you pack — only how early you should book excursions. If you want multiple dressy looks for a Valentine's sailing or simply prefer options, a checked bag is always fine; carry-on is just very achievable in February.

    February Caribbean Cruise Packing List

    Tops
    5–7 lightweight tops
    Tank tops, t-shirts, and linen or moisture-wicking shirts for warm, dry days. Quick-dry fabrics beat cotton, and most tops re-wear once after air-drying.
    Bottoms
    2–3 shorts, skirts, or light pants
    Shorts handle the warm days. One pair of light pants or a maxi skirt covers cooler evenings and excursions where longer coverage helps.
    Swimwear
    2 swimsuits + 1 cover-up
    Two suits let one dry while you wear the other. February water is comfortable across most itineraries and beaches are in full swing — pack one suit in your embarkation-day bag.
    Light Layer
    1 wrap, light cardigan, or packable hoodie
    February's one non-summer item — but a light one. Evenings are milder than January, so you rarely need a full jacket; a thin layer covers the ship's cold air conditioning and the occasional breezy night on deck, especially in the northern Caribbean.
    Dinner / Formal Wear
    1 dressy outfit
    Most 7-day sailings have one or two formal or elegant nights. One versatile outfit covers it on mainstream lines. A Valentine's or Presidents'-week cruise can feel slightly dressier, so consider one nicer option if you're sailing those weeks.
    Underwear & Socks
    5–7 underwear, 2–3 pairs socks
    Fewer socks than usual — you'll be in sandals or barefoot most of the trip. Socks mainly for walking shoes on excursion days and dressier evenings.
    Footwear
    Sandals (worn) + water shoes (packed)
    Sandals or flip-flops cover pool decks, the ship, and casual port walking. Water shoes or sport sandals with grip are essential for beach excursions — many Caribbean ports have coral or rocky entry points. Add dressier sandals or loafers if your line enforces a formal code.
    Sun Protection
    Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50+), sunglasses, hat
    February's clear, dry skies mean strong sun — the UV index runs high year-round in the Caribbean. Some destinations restrict sunscreens with reef-harming ingredients — Aruba restricts oxybenzone, and Bonaire restricts oxybenzone and octinoxate — so check your itinerary; a mineral sunscreen is a simple choice. Bring your own rather than paying the ship-shop markup. Add a wide-brim hat and polarized sunglasses.
    Daypack / Excursion Gear
    Small sling or daypack (8–15L)
    Doubles as your embarkation-day bag and port kit — room for sunscreen, water, a phone, your light layer, and a waterproof pouch, while staying light enough to carry all day. Book popular February excursions ahead; they sell out in peak season.

    Power & Tech
    Phone charger + power bank
    Cabin outlets are limited. A power bank keeps your phone alive on full-day port excursions. Cruise cabins prohibit surge-protected power strips — bring a non-surge outlet adapter if you need extra plugs.
    Documents & Meds
    Passport/ID, cruise documents, motion sickness meds, waterproof phone pouch
    Keep documents in your embarkation-day bag, never in checked luggage. February seas are generally calm, but tender ports and small-boat excursions can still trigger motion sickness — have relief accessible before you need it.

    Common Questions

    February Caribbean Cruise Packing FAQ

    What should I pack for a Caribbean cruise in February?
    Warm-weather basics — 5–7 light tops, 2–3 shorts or skirts, 2 swimsuits, a cover-up, sandals, water shoes, and reef-safe sunscreen — plus one light layer for the ship's air conditioning and mild evenings. A wrap or cardigan is enough; you rarely need a jacket in February. A Valentine's sailing may warrant one dressier option. Most setups pack into about 28–35L, and a 35–40L carry-on is the comfortable pick.
    Is February the warmest winter month for a Caribbean cruise?
    Close to it — February is among the warmest, driest, and calmest winter months, with low-to-mid 80s days (especially the Southern Caribbean), low humidity, and calm seas. It's a touch warmer than January with milder evenings, though the far-northern Caribbean and Bahamas can still cool off after dark. Water is comfortable for swimming across most itineraries.
    Is February a busy time for a Caribbean cruise?
    Yes — it's peak season. Great weather plus Presidents' Day week, Valentine's, and winter-escape travelers make February one of the busiest and priciest months to sail. Ships are fuller, popular excursions sell out earlier, and fares run higher than January. Book excursions ahead. For packing it changes little — just expect crowds at ports and pool decks.
    Do I still need a light layer for a February Caribbean cruise?
    Yes, but a lighter one than January. Days are warm and you'll live in shorts and swimwear, but the ship's air conditioning runs cold and evenings can cool off, more so in the northern Caribbean. A wrap, light cardigan, or packable hoodie handles it — a full jacket is rarely needed in February.
    Is February or January better for a Caribbean cruise?
    Both are excellent dry-season months. February is slightly warmer and calmer with milder evenings, but it's peak season — busier and pricier. January is one of the driest months with the fewest crowds and better post-holiday value, but cooler evenings. For packing, February needs a slightly lighter evening layer; everything else is nearly identical.

    Bottom Line

    • February is peak Caribbean season — one of the warmest, driest, and calmest winter months, with low-to-mid 80s days
    • The packing base stays light and warm-weather easy — one of the lowest-volume cruise months
    • Bring just one light layer — a wrap or cardigan for the ship's AC and mild evenings; a full jacket is rarely needed
    • A Valentine's or Presidents'-week sailing can feel slightly dressier — consider one nicer outfit
    • Reef-safe sunscreen, water shoes, and a light layer are the most-forgotten items
    • It's the busiest, priciest month — book excursions early, though it doesn't change what you pack
    • Carry-on only is very doable — a standard setup packs into ~28–35L, and a 35–40L bag is the comfortable pick; a checked bag is optional, not necessary
