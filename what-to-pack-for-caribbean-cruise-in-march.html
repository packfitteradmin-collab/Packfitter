Packing Guide — Caribbean Cruise in March

What to Pack for a Caribbean Cruise in March

Last updated: August 2026

March is a warm, sunny stretch of the Caribbean cruise season — warm water, strong tropical sun, and the late dry season still in full swing. It's also spring-break season, so ships and popular ports run busier, especially over mid-March. Packing is warm-weather easy: shorts, swimwear, and just a thin layer for the ship's air conditioning — the cooler evenings of January and February have eased off. The one thing to take seriously is sun protection. This guide covers what March changes, what most people forget, and how to fit it all in a carry-on.

Quick Answer

What Should I Pack for a Caribbean Cruise in March?

Pack warm-weather basics and lean into sun protection — March brings strong tropical sun and warm water. Days are warm and dry; you only need a thin layer for the ship's air conditioning, not a jacket. Standard sailings have one or two formal nights. The most forgotten items are reef-safe sunscreen, water shoes, and a hat — and booking excursions early for the spring-break crowds.

Quick Decision

Can You Do a March Caribbean Cruise with Just a Carry-On?

Yes — March is about as easy as cruise packing gets. It's one of the easier Caribbean cruise months for packing light: shorts, swimwear, and just a thin layer for the air conditioning, since the cooler evenings of mid-winter have faded. A standard setup packs into about 28–35L, and a 35–40L carry-on handles a 7-day sailing comfortably for most packers. The spring-break crowds don't change what you pack — only that you should book excursions early. If you want multiple dressy looks or simply prefer options, a checked bag is always fine; carry-on is just very achievable in March.

    March Caribbean Cruise Packing List

    Tops
    5–7 lightweight tops
    Tank tops, t-shirts, and linen or moisture-wicking shirts for warm, sunny days. Quick-dry fabrics beat cotton, and most tops re-wear once after air-drying.
    Bottoms
    2–3 shorts, skirts, or light pants
    Shorts handle the warm days. One pair of light pants or a maxi skirt covers dinner and excursions where longer coverage helps.
    Swimwear
    2 swimsuits + 1 cover-up
    March water is warm and comfortable for swimming, so pool and beach time is prime. Two suits let one dry while you wear the other; pack one in your embarkation-day bag for the pool deck.
    Sun Protection
    Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50+), sun hat, sunglasses
    March's biggest priority. The sun is strong and UV is high, and the sea breeze hides how much you're getting. Some Caribbean destinations restrict sunscreens with reef-harming ingredients — Aruba restricts oxybenzone, and Bonaire restricts oxybenzone and octinoxate — so check your itinerary; a mineral sunscreen is a simple choice. Bring your own either way to avoid steep onboard prices, and add a wide-brim hat and polarized sunglasses.
    Thin Layer
    1 wrap, light cardigan, or packable hoodie
    The one non-summer item, and lighter than the deeper-winter months call for. Evenings are mild in March, so this is mainly for the ship's cold air conditioning indoors — a full jacket isn't needed.
    Dinner / Formal Wear
    1 dressy outfit
    Most 7-day sailings have one or two formal or elegant nights. One versatile outfit — a collared shirt or blouse with dress pants, or a wrap dress — covers it on mainstream lines.
    Underwear & Socks
    5–7 underwear, 2–3 pairs socks
    Fewer socks than usual — you'll be in sandals or barefoot most of the trip. Socks mainly for walking shoes on excursion days and dressier evenings.
    Footwear
    Sandals (worn) + water shoes (packed)
    Sandals or flip-flops cover pool decks, the ship, and casual port walking. Water shoes or sport sandals with grip are essential for beach excursions — many Caribbean ports have coral or rocky entry points. Add dressier sandals or loafers if your line enforces a formal code.
    Daypack / Excursion Gear
    Small sling or daypack (8–15L)
    Doubles as your embarkation-day bag and port kit — room for sunscreen, water, a phone, and a waterproof pouch, while staying light enough to carry all day. Book popular March excursions ahead; spring-break weeks sell out.

    Power & Tech
    Phone charger + power bank
    Cabin outlets are limited. A power bank keeps your phone alive on full-day port excursions. Cruise cabins prohibit surge-protected power strips — bring a non-surge outlet adapter if you need extra plugs.
    Documents & Meds
    Passport/ID, cruise documents, motion sickness meds, waterproof phone pouch
    Keep documents in your embarkation-day bag, never in checked luggage. March seas are generally calm, but tender ports and small-boat excursions can still trigger motion sickness — have relief accessible before you need it.

    Common Questions

    March Caribbean Cruise Packing FAQ

    What should I pack for a Caribbean cruise in March?
    Warm-weather basics — 5–7 light tops, 2–3 shorts or skirts, 2 swimsuits, a cover-up, sandals, water shoes — plus serious sun protection: reef-safe sunscreen SPF 50+, a hat, and sunglasses, since March sun is strong and UV is high. Add just a thin layer for the ship's air conditioning; a jacket isn't needed. Most setups pack into about 28–35L, and a 35–40L carry-on is the comfortable pick.
    Is the water warm enough to swim on a March Caribbean cruise?
    Yes — March water is warm and comfortable for swimming and snorkeling, typically high 70s to low 80s°F, with the Southern Caribbean generally warmer than northern routes such as the Bahamas. Pack two swimsuits and keep one in your embarkation-day bag for the pool deck.
    Is a March Caribbean cruise crowded with spring break?
    Often, yes. March is spring-break season, so sailings — especially mid-March and school-break weeks — draw families and students, and ports run busier. It doesn't change what you pack, but book excursions and specialty dining early. Early March or a Southern Caribbean itinerary tends to be calmer than the Bahamas and Eastern Caribbean hotspots.
    How strong is the sun on a March Caribbean cruise?
    Strong — the UV index is high year-round and climbs through spring, so March sun is intense on open decks and beaches, and the sea breeze hides how much you're getting. A high-SPF sunscreen is essential, plus a wide-brim hat and polarized sunglasses. Some destinations restrict sunscreens with reef-harming ingredients (for example, Aruba restricts oxybenzone and Bonaire restricts oxybenzone and octinoxate), so a mineral sunscreen is a simple choice — check your itinerary. Bring your own; onboard prices are steep, and reapply after swimming.
    Is it too warm for a jacket on a March Caribbean cruise?
    Essentially, yes. March days are firmly warm-weather and evenings are mild, so you don't need a jacket — just a thin layer (a wrap, cardigan, or packable hoodie) for the ship's cold air conditioning. That's a step lighter than January or February, when cooler evenings make a layer more useful.

    Bottom Line

    • March is a warm, sunny Caribbean cruise month — warm water, strong tropical sun, and the late dry season
    • The packing base is light — warm-weather clothing and barely any layers
    • Sun protection is the priority: reef-safe sunscreen SPF 50+, a hat, and sunglasses matter more than extra clothes
    • Bring only a thin layer for the ship's air conditioning — evenings are mild and a jacket isn't needed
    • It's spring-break season — book excursions and dining early, though it doesn't change what you pack
    • Water is warm — double up on swimwear rather than clothing
    • Carry-on only is very doable — a standard setup packs into ~28–35L, and a 35–40L bag is the comfortable pick; a checked bag is optional, not necessary
